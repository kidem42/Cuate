# Penumbral Systematics

*A visual philosophy. Everything else in this folder is downstream of it.*

---

## The movement

Penumbral Systematics holds that the most truthful place to look at any body is not its
lit face and not its shadow, but the narrow band where the two negotiate. The umbra is
absolute and tells us nothing. Full illumination flattens and tells us less. Meaning
gathers at the edge — in the penumbra, that thin territory of partial occlusion where
form finally declares its curvature. The movement takes this band as its entire subject
and treats it with the patience a cartographer brings to a coastline. Nothing here is
gestural. Every arc is the consequence of two positions and one radius, and the work
should read as though someone spent a very long time making sure of it.

## Space and form

The circle is the only primitive, and it is never drawn alone. Two circles of identical
radius, displaced by a small distance, generate every form the movement recognises:
crescent, lens, ring, chord, gap. Nothing is added that cannot be derived. Composition
proceeds by displacement rather than by invention — the artist moves one body relative to
another and accepts what the geometry returns. This is a discipline of constraint, and
the constraint is the point: a vocabulary this narrow can only be made to sing through
master-level judgement about proportion, and every millimetre of that judgement must be
visible in the finished plate.

## Colour and material

Two temperatures, one ground. A warm light and a cool light fall on the same body from
opposing sides, and the body itself stays near-black — not the flat black of ink but the
slightly violet black of volcanic glass, which holds a faint interior life when it catches
a gradient. Colour is scarce by law. The warm accounts for a few percent of any surface,
the cool for less, and the remainder is ground. Scarcity is what makes a colour read as
light rather than as paint. Every value in the palette must be derivable, measurable,
defensible; nothing is chosen because it looked nice on the day.

## Scale and rhythm

The movement's characteristic gesture is the systematic array: one form, repeated across
a grid, varying by a single parameter per axis. The reward is cumulative. A viewer takes
in the field at a glance, then discovers that no two cells are identical, then discovers
the rule, then finds the one cell where the rule produces something the others do not.
This is slow looking, engineered. Beneath the array sits a single large specimen — the
same form at full scale, so the eye can travel between the general case and the
particular. Rhythm comes from the interval between cells, and that interval must be tuned
with painstaking attention: too tight and the field reads as texture, too loose and it
reads as a list.

## Composition and hierarchy

The plate is the native format: generous margins, hairline rules, corner registration
marks, axis ticks. It borrows the visual grammar of scientific observation and applies it
to something that cannot strictly be observed, and the seriousness of that borrowing is
what gives the work its charge. Typography is instrumental, never decorative — a
geometric sans for the one display word, a monospace for every label, set small,
letterspaced, and placed exactly where a caption would go on a real specimen sheet. Text
never explains. It annotates. If a paragraph appears, the composition has failed.

## Craft

This is work that must look laboured over. Not fussy — laboured over. The arcs must
terminate cleanly, the gaps must be equal where equality is claimed and deliberately
unequal where it is not, the type must sit on a baseline that was decided rather than
defaulted. Someone at the top of their field made this, over many hours, and refused to
ship it until the last hairline was where it belonged. Any element that cannot survive
that standard should be removed rather than improved, because in a vocabulary this narrow
the only real luxury is restraint — and restraint, executed at this level, is the most
difficult thing in the discipline.
